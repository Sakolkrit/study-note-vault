# GAN 2014

> **In one line for a 12-year-old:** Two networks play a game. A forger tries to make fake pictures, and a detective tries to spot the fakes. Each gets better because of the other.

## The problem
Learn to make new data that looks like the training data.

## What people did before
Generative models that needed Markov chains or unrolled approximate inference networks (the abstract says GANs do not need them). See [[Generative Models]].

## The idea
Train a generator G and a discriminator D together as a minimax game: D estimates the chance that a sample came from the training data, and G is trained to make D wrong ([[GAN]]).

## What they found
In the space of arbitrary functions, the abstract says a unique solution exists, with G recovering the training distribution and D equal to 1/2 everywhere. With multilayer perceptrons the whole system trains with backpropagation (abstract). The experiments are described as qualitative and quantitative evaluation of samples; I did not read any numbers.

## Why it mattered
It gave a new way to train a generator without a likelihood, and sharp image generation followed from it.

## Limits and open questions
Training can be unstable, and a generator can cover only part of the data ('mode collapse'; from memory, and shown in the vault's toy in [[GAN]]). The abstract does not discuss this.
