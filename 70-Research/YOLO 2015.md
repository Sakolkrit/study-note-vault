# YOLO 2015

> **In one line for a 12-year-old:** Look at the whole picture once and write down all the boxes and labels straight away, like glancing at a room and listing everything you see.

## The problem
Detection fast enough for video.

## What people did before
Prior work repurposed classifiers for detection (the abstract), as in [[R-CNN 2013]] with its many separate steps.

## The idea
Frame detection as a regression problem to spatially separated boxes and class probabilities. A single network predicts them in one evaluation, trained end to end.

## What they found
Base YOLO runs at 45 frames per second (about 22 milliseconds per image, my arithmetic), and Fast YOLO at 155 frames per second with double the mAP of other real-time detectors. It makes more localisation errors but fewer false detections on empty background, and generalises better from photos to artwork (abstract).

## Why it mattered
Real-time detection became practical: video, robots, phones.

## Limits and open questions
More localisation mistakes than the slower systems (abstract). Later YOLO versions changed much; I did not read them.
