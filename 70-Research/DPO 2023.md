# DPO 2023

> **In one line for a 12-year-old:** The old way needed two stages and a lot of tuning to teach a model what people prefer. This paper found a trick that does it with one simple learning step on pairs of better and worse answers.

## The problem
RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure (abstract): first fit a reward model, then use reinforcement learning to maximise it without drifting too far from the original model.

## What people did before
[[RLHF]] with a separate [[Reward Model]] and [[PPO]], as in [[InstructGPT 2022]].

## The idea
A new parameterization of the reward model that makes the optimal policy available in closed form, so the standard RLHF problem is solved with only a simple classification loss ([[Direct Preference Optimization]], [[Bradley-Terry Model]]).

## What they found
The abstract says DPO is stable, performant and lightweight, with no sampling from the model during fine-tuning and little hyperparameter tuning. It exceeds PPO-based RLHF in controlling sentiment, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler (abstract).

## Why it mattered
It made preference training much easier to run, so it spread quickly.

## Limits and open questions
The results in the abstract are on sentiment control, summarization and single-turn dialogue, so how it behaves on other tasks is a question the abstract does not answer.

## Concepts used
[[Direct Preference Optimization]], [[RLHF]], [[Reward Model]], [[Bradley-Terry Model]], [[Loss Function]]
