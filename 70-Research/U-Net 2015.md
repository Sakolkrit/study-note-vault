# U-Net 2015

> **In one line for a 12-year-old:** A network shaped like the letter U: it shrinks the picture to understand it, then grows it back to the original size, with shortcuts that carry fine detail across. It works even when you have very few labelled pictures.

## The problem
Segment biomedical images when only a small number of annotated examples is available (the abstract opens by noting the belief that thousands of annotated samples are needed).

## What people did before
A sliding-window convolutional network, which the abstract says was the prior best method on the ISBI challenge, and [[FCN 2014]].

## The idea
A contracting path to capture context and a symmetric expanding path that enables precise localization, trained end to end with strong use of [[Data Augmentation]] to use the few annotated samples more efficiently ([[Image Segmentation]]).

## What they found
It can be trained from very few images and outperforms the prior best method (a sliding-window convolutional network) on the ISBI challenge for segmentation of neuronal structures in electron microscopic stacks. It won the ISBI cell tracking challenge 2015 in the phase contrast and DIC categories by a large margin, and segments a 512 by 512 image in under a second on a recent GPU (abstract).

## Why it mattered
It became the standard design for medical and many other segmentation tasks (from memory).

## Limits and open questions
The abstract describes biomedical images only; how it fares on other data is outside what I read.

## Concepts used
[[Image Segmentation]], [[Convolutional Neural Network]], [[Data Augmentation]], [[Autoencoder]], [[Convolution]]

## Reading trail
- **Came from:** [[FCN 2014]]
- **Led to:** [[Mask R-CNN 2017]] (instance segmentation, a different design)
