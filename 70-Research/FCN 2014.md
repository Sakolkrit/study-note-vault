# FCN 2014

> **In one line for a 12-year-old:** Take a picture-recognising network and remove its last dense layers, so that it gives an answer for every pixel instead of one answer for the whole picture.

## The problem
Label every pixel of a picture (semantic segmentation).

## What people did before
Classification networks that output one label per picture, and earlier segmentation methods built on patches (the abstract mentions connections to prior models).

## The idea
Fully convolutional networks: convolutional networks trained end to end, pixels to pixels, that accept input of any size and produce correspondingly-sized output. Contemporary classification networks (AlexNet, the VGG net and GoogLeNet) are adapted and fine-tuned for segmentation, and a new architecture combines a deep, coarse layer's semantic information with a shallow, fine layer's appearance information ([[Image Segmentation]], [[Fine-tuning]], [[Transfer Learning]]).

## What they found
State-of-the-art segmentation of PASCAL VOC (a 20% relative improvement to 62.2% mean IU on 2012), NYUDv2 and SIFT Flow, with inference in one third of a second for a typical image (abstract).

## Why it mattered
It made 'pixels to pixels' the usual way to do segmentation (from memory), and its idea of combining coarse and fine layers was refined in [[U-Net 2015]].

## Limits and open questions
The output is coarse unless the fine layers are combined (the abstract's own reason for the new architecture); small structures are still hard (from memory).

## Concepts used
[[Image Segmentation]], [[Convolutional Neural Network]], [[VGG]], [[Transfer Learning]], [[Fine-tuning]]

## Reading trail
- **Came from:** [[VGG 2014]], [[GoogLeNet 2014]], [[AlexNet 2012]]
- **Led to:** [[U-Net 2015]], [[Mask R-CNN 2017]]
