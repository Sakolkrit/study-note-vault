# VGG 2014

> **In one line for a 12-year-old:** Instead of big filters, stack lots of tiny 3-by-3 filters and go deeper. Deeper turned out to be better.

## The problem
How much does depth help a convolutional network?

## What people did before
[[LeNet and AlexNet]] with bigger first filters and 8 layers.

## The idea
Use only very small 3x3 [[Convolution]] filters, and push the depth to 16 to 19 weight layers.

## What they found
The abstract says depth 16 to 19 gives a significant improvement over earlier configurations. The team took 1st place in localisation and 2nd in classification in ILSVRC 2014.

## Why it mattered
It showed that depth, with a simple repeated design, was the main lever. The two best models were released publicly and reused widely as feature extractors ([[Transfer Learning]]).

## Limits and open questions
Very large in parameters and slow (from memory; not measured here). Beyond about 20 layers, plain stacks got hard to train, see [[ResNet]].

## Concepts used
[[Convolution]], [[Receptive Field]], [[Image Classification]], [[VGG]]

## Reading trail
- **Came from:** [[AlexNet 2012]]
- **Led to:** [[ResNet 2015]] and [[FCN 2014]] (and [[GoogLeNet 2014]], from the same contest)
